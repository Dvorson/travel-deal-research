# Flight research patterns

Treat these as candidate experiments, not promises of savings. Refresh schedules, fees and eligibility for the actual trip.

## High-value baseline and expansion

1. **Search the real party first.** Use exact adult/child/infant categories and required bags. Compare one traveller versus the full party only to diagnose pricing structure. Reprice any valid grouping with children and adults correctly allocated. Never assume single-seat inventory exists for everyone or group pricing must be worse.
2. **Explore dates and duration together.** Screen outbound × return within permitted nights; reprice distinct promising windows and calculate stay costs for each. Test duration thresholds only within leave/school constraints.
3. **Inspect more than default rankings.** Recommended flights may trade money for convenience. Check low-price lists, filters and omitted options. Google documents different Best/Cheapest lists and incomplete coverage [1].
4. **Price round trip and independent one-ways.** Inspect mixed carriers and different return gateways. Independent outbound/return tickets are distinct from a same-day self-connection. Neither construction always wins.
5. **Price open-jaw/multicity for multiple places.** Compare avoided backtracking with added ground/car cost. Price whole trips and directional subroutes; airline segment pricing is not always additive.
6. **Search reachable alternative airports.** Include actual home-to-terminal time/cost, parking, fuel/tolls, late transport and any hotel. Consider cross-border airports when acceptable, not just a radius. Resolve physical airports within city codes.
7. **Check direct low-cost/leisure carriers.** Use airport route maps and seasonal schedules to discover suppliers missing from the initial metasearch. A route map is not dated inventory. Include relevant tour-operator flight-only sales.
8. **Decompose difficult routes.** Search from both ends toward plausible hubs; compare through-ticket pricing before permitted separate tickets. Use targeted ITA routing/date searches, not an assumption that its broadest query is exhaustive [2].

## Conditional branches

- **Self-transfer/repositioning:** Check reclaim/recheck, landside/entry eligibility, terminal changes, operating hours and a scenario-specific buffer. Include overnight costs and the actual protection contract. An OTA guarantee need not equal through-ticket protection. Keep this branch separate if its risks are not accepted.
- **Stopovers:** Compare only if the traveller welcomes one; verify fare eligibility, accommodation benefits, transfer cost and actual dates. A promotional hotel is not an entitlement until its terms and inventory are checked.
- **Packages:** Compare flight-plus-hotel and leisure-operator inventory with the same times, bags, room, meals and transfers. Verify totals independently of discount claims.
- **Market/currency:** Test a few legitimately available sales markets when plausible. Match eligibility, taxes, card charges, FX rate and refund currency. Language does not establish another fare market. Never misstate residency or promise VPN savings.
- **Rewards:** Use supplied/eligible programmes or a requested rewards strategy. Verify full-party award seats, surcharges, transfers, expiry, cancellation and points consumed. Do not treat points as free, transfers as reversible, or transfer/buy them during research.
- **Fare history/alerts:** Treat route-specific current evidence as context, not certainty. Historical booking windows are market-specific. Schedule future checks only when requested.

## Exclude unsupported folklore

Do not promise that Tuesday purchases, incognito, clearing cookies, currency changes or VPNs lower prices. Distinguish day of travel from day of purchase. Validate claimed savings by like-for-like repricing. Treat flash/error-fare posts as perishable leads. Exclude hidden-city/throwaway tickets from the default; their fare conditions and baggage/remaining-segment implications differ.

## Evidence and identity

Record each segment's carrier, flight number, airports, local dates/times and zones when available. Record fare brand, baggage, protection and seller. Deduplicate itinerary identity separately from seller offers; approximate departure time is insufficient.

Keep cached calendar prices as leads. Poll asynchronous sources to completion or disclose partial results; Skyscanner documents that its first response is incomplete and better itineraries can arrive later [3]. Final comparisons need selected offers and applicable terms, without initiating bookings.
